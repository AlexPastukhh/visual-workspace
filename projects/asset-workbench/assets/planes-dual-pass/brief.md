# Dual aircraft — 2D scene brief

Create a clean, readable top-down 2D flight scene with two distinct aircraft.

## Desired action

- The upper aircraft begins on the left and flies horizontally to the right.
- The lower aircraft begins on the right, clearly below the upper aircraft, and flies horizontally to the left.
- Both reach opposite ends, perform a visible 180-degree turnaround, then fly back along their own lanes.
- The upper aircraft returns right-to-left; the lower aircraft returns left-to-right.
- The planes must face the direction in which they are travelling throughout outbound and return travel. During the turnaround, facing rotates continuously rather than snapping.
- Keep vertical ordering and clear separation. Do not cross or overlap the aircraft.

## Visual direction

Use a polished, stylized top-down aircraft illustration. The upper plane uses warm orange/red colours; the lower plane uses cool cyan/blue colours. Keep silhouettes distinct, with readable wings, fuselage, cockpit and nose. The background should provide contrast without competing with the aircraft.

## Acceptance intent

The complete scene must be understandable at 1280×720. Both aircraft remain visible. The two outbound trajectories are opposite; the return trajectories are opposite to their respective outbound trajectories. The turnaround is visible and continuous. No unexplained pause, teleport, sudden facing flip or change of lane.

## Review emphasis

Check the whole shared composition first, then track each actor independently. Pay particular attention to direction versus facing, timing of the turnaround, vertical ordering and spacing.
